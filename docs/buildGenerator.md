This page explains how the build generator works: what it searches, how it scores a build, how it uses several processor cores, and how to measure a change to it. The window is opened from the Build Ideas button ("Generate Build"); the code starts in [`BuildGenerator:Run()`](../src/Classes/BuildGenerator.lua).

#### Scoring
Every decision is made by [`BuildScorer`](../src/Classes/BuildScorer.lua) on PoB's own calculator output, so a build is only as good as PoB says it is.

- **Damage**: the main skill's DPS (combined parts for skills like Kinetic Blast), reduced when the skill can't be paid for in a long fight.
- **Tankiness**: mostly the maximum hit taken of the weakest damage type, then all damage types, a little EHP, and how many times over the build takes the toughest pinnacle (or uber) boss hit.
- **Defence targets**: past its target (the max hit: 2.5x the focus's floor; the boss hit: taken twice over; avoidance: half the hit damage avoided) each layer counts for a third, so damage isn't traded for tankiness the build doesn't need.
- **Boss fight**: the pressure between a pinnacle boss's big hits (its regular hits and ground effects, a tenth of its worst hit a second, less what is avoided) against what the build recovers, simulated over a minute: dying before the end costs score. Our Righteous Fire build held 40 seconds; the top players' builds the whole fight.
- **Avoidance**: the share of incoming hit damage the build takes on average (melee, projectile, spell and spell projectile hits) after evasion, dodge, block and spell suppression, with its own weight in every focus. Top players build max block or 100% suppression and value it about as much as doubling their damage; inside EHP it was worth a few % damage.
- **Focus**: the weights of damage and tankiness. "Damage, with a minimum max hit" (`DPS_FLOOR`) is damage first with a 10,000 max hit floor; below the floor every missing bit costs more than damage can pay back.
- **Penalties**: elemental resistances below the cap, missing attributes, reservation over 100% (more the further over), self-damage over time bigger than regeneration, a defence checklist (freeze, shock, bleed, poison, stun...) and drawbacks PoB doesn't calculate ([`GeneratorRules`](../src/Modules/GeneratorRules.lua)).
- **Passives PoB doesn't calculate**: cluster notables whose effect PoB can't read (Fan the Flames' ignite spread, Blast-Freeze, Remarkable's extra brands, Purposeful Harbinger...) get a value for the build ([`GeneratorRules`](../src/Modules/GeneratorRules.lua) `uncountedNodes`): ignite spread is worth a lot to an ignite build without Ignite Proliferation, nothing to a hit build.
- **Smooth play**: only buffs that are always up count in full. Vaal skills and guard skills are switched off, buffs that stay where they are cast (Sigil of Power) aren't used, and a flask that comes and goes counts for less than its average uptime (none below half the time).
- **Flasks**: their real uptime from a simulated fight (used whenever down and charged, charges lost at the maximum, none gained while up with an Enkindling enchantment); tinctures from their mana burn.

The score is logarithmic in damage and in the max hit, so doubling the damage is worth the same at 1 million as at 100 million.

**Calibrated on the top players' builds** (`bench/penalty_census.py` over their evaluations): a penalty that fires on many builds that work in the game is wrong. Three were, and were changed:
- *Paying for the skill*: a quarter of the top builds "couldn't pay" for their main skill. PoB's cost per second assumes totems and brands are placed at their placement / cast speed (a Ballista build re-placing its totems 6 times a second); they are now placed again only when they expire. A build must also recover 80% of its cost, not all of it (flasks, pauses and positioning cover the rest).
- *Uber boss hits*: half the top builds survive less than 58% of the worst uber hit (they dodge the telegraphed ones). Below 60% the penalty is unchanged; from 60% to surviving it, it is light. Pinnacle (non-uber) hits still have to be survived.
- *Socket colours*: gems an item can't realistically roll (six blue gems in a strength body armour) cost score, from the chromatic odds of the base (weights 12 + the attribute requirement), with the crafting bench forcing three sockets and white sockets fitting anything; from 1 in 1,000 rolls on. None of the top builds checked had any.

#### Ideas, priorities and learning
- **Ideas from the game data** ([`IdeaMiner`](../src/Classes/IdeaMiner.lua)): uniques whose lines scale with a stat ("per 1% Chance to Block", "per Power Charge", "per 500 Evasion Rating"...) make that stat an idea to stack, scanned like the written archetypes, with the stat lens and the planner's investment in that stat.
- **Learnt priorities** ([`GeneratorLearning`](../src/Modules/GeneratorLearning.lua)): a quick build ranks archetypes by how they start, but a full build gains more over it for some (mines +4.2, physical weapons +3.6) than others (Dexterity stacking +2.2). Archetypes are fully built in the order of quick score + learnt gain + a bonus for little-tried ones (an upper confidence bound, as bandit algorithms use), from the benchmark (`Data/GeneratorPriors.json`) and every automatic generation on the computer (`GeneratorLearning.json` in the user folder).
- **Alternatives**: the automatic mode keeps every idea's full build; those within 1 point of the best are listed and can be opened.
- **Explanation**: each build says what it is built around and what carries it (each part taken out alone: the damage and max hit it would lose).

#### The main skill as used in a fight
PoB shows one skill part at a time and leaves counts like Toxic Rain's overlapping pods at 1. [`SkillProfiles`](../src/Classes/SkillProfiles.lua) sets what a player realistically gets on a pinnacle boss (and while mapping), for about 30 of the most played skills: the part shown (Tornado Shot's combined projectiles, Ball Lightning's bolts in range, Explosive Arrow's sustainable fuses...), the best element (Elemental Hit), counts (3 Toxic Rain pods or Storm Rain beams overlapping, Perforate spikes, Shrapnel Ballista projectiles), parts that add up on one target (Earthquake's impact and aftershock, Kinetic Blast's projectile and 2 explosions, Glacial Cascade's bursts, a third of Spark's possible hits), stacks kept up (Blade Vortex blades), conditions (Cyclone channelled). The assumption is noted in the log ("Main skill as used in a fight"). Boneshatter's trauma is PoB's own sustainable trauma.

#### Archetypes
An archetype ([`BuildArchetypes`](../src/Classes/BuildArchetypes.lua)) is what the generator commits to before optimising everything else around it: keystones (crit, Elemental Overload, Resolute Technique, Chaos Inoculation, Avatar of Fire...), auras and supports (Pride + Brutality for impale, Hatred for physical weapons), uniques that scale with a stat (mana, intelligence, strength, dexterity, life) and playstyles (mines, traps, totems). The scan builds a quick version of every archetype that fits the skill; the automatic mode then builds the best three fully and keeps the best one.

Trigger archetypes ([`TriggerSetups`](../src/Classes/TriggerSetups.lua)) apply to spells that can be triggered: Cast while Channelling (a channelling skill such as Cyclone or Flameblast), Cast On Critical Strike (an attack such as Cyclone, Ice Shot or Power Siphon), Arcanist Brand, and Spellslinger (a wand attack in a link of its own). The spell stays the main skill of the link, with the trigger skill and support linked after it and kept there whatever supports change, so PoB counts its damage at the trigger rate (`Modules/CalcTriggers`). The trigger skill is the one that scores best with the weapons it can use. On quick test builds they score below self-cast (Ice Nova: self-cast 15.55, Cast while Channelling 14.04, Spellslinger 13.80, Arcanist Brand 13.20, Cast On Critical Strike 12.02 without crit investment), as PoB sees them: their worth is mostly playing while moving.

Stat stacking archetypes (Intelligence, Strength, Dexterity, mana, life, energy shield) commit to their stat: while the build is made, the stat counts on its own in the score (a "lens"), so rares, passives, jewels and uniques follow it before the items that pay it back are all in place. The final score leaves the lens out, so archetypes are compared as they are. On a Ball Lightning Intelligence build this doubled the damage of the finished build.

A quick build ranks archetypes by how they start, so the scan also runs the cheap late steps that change the ranking the most (every affordable unique, a Watcher's Eye for the archetype's auras, the supports again). The automatic mode fully builds the best three, and up to five when more start within 0.5 of the best one; archetypes whose quick builds came out the same (one that adds nothing usable) are built once.

#### The pipeline
1. Configuration from what the build does ([`AutoConfig`](../src/Classes/AutoConfig.lua)): charges it generates, ailments it keeps up, conditions it meets.
2. Supports for the main link, one at a time, plus the archetype's and the playstyle's.
3. Passive tree: greedy by score gained per point, with the best candidates re-checked before each pick ("lazy greedy"); masteries as their own candidates; ascendancy and bloodline notables.
4. Item bases, the archetype's uniques.
5. Rare items ([`RareItemGenerator`](../src/Classes/RareItemGenerator.lua)): every mod that can roll on the base is scored alone, then the item is filled one mod at a time; one crafting bench mod; influences and Exarch / Eater implicits by budget.
6. Supports again; curse, aura, guard, movement setups linked like real builds ([`GemSetup`](../src/Classes/GemSetup.lua)).
7. Tree again for the gear.
8. Influenced rares.
9. Every affordable unique in every slot, poe.ninja's popular uniques, rare jewels, timeless jewels, Forbidden Flame / Flesh, Watcher's Eye (mods tried in pairs and triples), Split Personality, jewels that allocate passives in their radius without a path ([`RadiusJewelFinder`](../src/Classes/RadiusJewelFinder.lua): Thread of Hope in every ring size and nearby socket, Intuitive Leap, Impossible Escape for every keystone; each passive in reach valued once, each placement estimated against what a passive point is worth in the build, the best three applied for real with the tree trimmed and refilled), cluster jewels (Large ones, then Medium or Small ones in the sockets they add), anoint, tattoos.
10. Flasks (unique ones too, Enkindling enchantments), pantheon, bandit, aura stacking (auras chosen with reservation and costs ignored, the tree re-spent, what still can't be paid for dropped), the main link filled, the rares made again for the finished build.
11. Unique combos ([`UniqueComboFinder`](../src/Classes/UniqueComboFinder.lua)): the most promising uniques of each gear slot (one at a time, slightly worse ones included) scored two at a time in different slots, and pairs where one unique grants a state (Elusive, Onslaught, Unholy Might, Arcane Surge, Phasing, Adrenaline, Consecrated Ground...) and the other pays off in it ("while Elusive"...) with that state switched on. Pairs gaining more together than apart are applied with the build adapted, and kept only if they beat the same adaptation without them. AutoConfig keeps such a state on when the build has a line granting it reliably (a chance on hit at the main skill's hit rate; from kills only while mapping).
    Optional: unique / keystone ideas ([`SynergyFinder`](../src/Classes/SynergyFinder.lua)), packages the top players use together, the top players' gear and main link tried in the build, the passives most top players of the class take (read from their PoB codes) allocated as a set with the tree trimmed around them.
12. Ascendancy and supports checked again (each support swapped against every other one), the tree rebuilt with shorter paths ([`TreeOptimizer`](../src/Classes/TreeOptimizer.lua)), then a short second round (aura stacking, supports, ascendancy) on the rebuilt tree.

13. Rebuild and repair (large neighbourhood search): the finished build is partly undone and rebuilt, again and again, each round kept only if the build is better: the weakest branches of the passive tree pruned as a whole (10 to 20 points, by score lost per point; they can't grow straight back that round) and the points re-spent; one or two rares made again; two supports chosen again. Rares and passives are also made again in turns before that, until neither gains.

Every late move is kept only when the full score (with flask uptimes) is better. Bought items must gain more the more they cost (up to 0.04 at the investment level's item limit), and resistances or attributes still missing at the end are fixed by swapping a rare's bench craft.

Items and gems follow what can be found at the investment level, not only prices: below High investment there are no exceptional gems (Enlighten, Empower, Enhance) and no scarce uniques (Split Personality, Watcher's Eye, Cinderswallow Urn, Forbidden jewels... `PriceSource.scarceUniques`). Gem setups always fit the items' real sockets: uniques with a fixed count (Crest of Desire's single socket) or abyssal sockets pack the setups again, and a main link that no longer fits moves to a 4-link. Items whose granted supports change what a gem does there (traps, totems, triggers: `behaviourSupports`, from `bench/unique_audit.lua`) get no utility setup.

An Animate Guardian (from High investment, when sockets are free) wears the uniques that buff nearby allies best for the build (Kingmaker, Mask of the Tribunal, Replica Leer Cast, Victario's Flight...): their ally lines become the player's own in a Custom Modifier group of the configuration ("Animate Guardian (generated)"), where PoB counts them. On the Boneshatter test build: Kingmaker and Mask of the Tribunal, +0.16.

**Boss fight as played** (`BuildScorer:FightUptime`, on whenever the build is configured for a boss): a fifth of a boss fight is spent moving at normal movement speed (less when faster), and each big telegraphed hit (one every 10 seconds) the build can't take is dodged for 0.8 seconds (none when it takes the hit twice over, all of it below 60%). While moving, only damage that doesn't need the player keeps going: totems, brands, traps, mines, minions, a channelled skill that moves (Cyclone), and damage over time. The main skill's DPS is multiplied by the share kept, so movement speed, tankiness and persistent damage are worth what they are in a real fight; the log says how much ("Boss fight as played: 86% of the damage kept..."). On our Endgame builds: 85% for Winter Orb and Ethereal Knives (self-cast hits), 100% for traps, brands, Cyclone and poison Flicker Strike.

**League economy** ("Economy" in the window, `PriceSource.phases`): today's poe.ninja prices, or prices for league start (days 1-3), the first week, mid league or late league. Prices move from today's point of the league (from poe.ninja's league start date when it gives one, else mid league) along curves per kind of item, relative to mid league: chase uniques (20+ div) x5 at league start, x2.5 in the first week, x0.7 late; other uniques x2.5 / x1.6 / x0.8; cheap uniques x1.5 / x1.2; rares, anoints, gems and tattoos (crafting currency) x2 / x1.5 / x0.8. In the first week the scarce uniques (Mageblood, Headhunter, Forbidden jewels...) aren't used at any budget.

**Rare prices** (`PriceSource:RarePrice`): poe.ninja doesn't price rares, so each one is estimated from what it has: the level's typical rare (0.05 to 150 div) scaled by its good mods against what the level expects (squared), influences (x1.5 for one, x3 for two), Eldritch implicits (x1.3), extra bench crafts (+2 div each), the slot (weapons x1.8, body armours x1.2) and the league phase.

**Supreme Ostentation** (Elegant Hubris with Caspiro, from High investment) only pays once the build is rebuilt without attribute requirements, so it is tried as a package: the jewel (replacing the timeless jewel already chosen, or in a socket near a keystone), the passives taken for attributes spent again, the rares made again without attribute mods, the tree's targets re-chosen; kept if the whole is better. On the Ethereal Knives and Winter Orb test builds it came within 0.01 of the build as it was, so it stayed out.

**Stat stacking plan**: a stacking archetype's stat is worth more the more of it there is, so its value at the start undersells it. After the archetype's uniques are in, and again after the first gear, the generator measures what doubling the stat is worth against ten times its first tenth, and raises the lens's weight to that difference when it is bigger (up to 1; a linear payoff keeps the usual 0.3, lowering it lost 0.6 score on a Spark Intelligence build): the build is steered to the target, not the next point. The plan is in the log ("Stat plan").

**Total budget** ("Total div" in the window, optional): the whole build's cost in divines. It picks the investment level (up to 3 div League start, 15 Budget, 100 Mid, 450 High, 2000 Endgame), caps one item at half of it, and at the end fits the build under it: the parts worth the least score per divine (uniques for a rare on the same base, anoints, awakened gems for the normal ones, rares for ones two levels cheaper) are made cheaper until it fits. Rares are estimated from their mods (below), uniques at their cheapest poe.ninja copy (chosen mods and seeds cost more), awakened and exceptional gems at poe.ninja's gem prices.

**Upgrade order**: every full build ends with what each bought part is worth (the build with its cheap version instead) sorted by score per divine, with the build's cost after each step: what to buy first when building it up from a smaller budget.

**Upgrade open build** (button beside Generate; `BuildGenerator:Upgrade`, `bench/upgrade_build.lua`): the open build, with its own main skill, class, tree, gear and gems, is the start instead of an empty character. A copy opens and the late steps run on it within the investment level or total budget, each change kept only if the build scores better: supports, ascendancy, every affordable unique, rares made for the build, unique and permanent flasks, jewels in empty sockets, timeless and unique jewels, anoint, tattoos, pantheon, the tree rebuilt, damage behind conditions, unique combos, rebuild-and-repair rounds; then the budget fit and the upgrade order. The log lists every change with its gain and cost, and the numbers before and after. Our 30-div Cyclone of Tumult Slayer upgraded to a 150-div budget: 2.2M to 6.5M DPS, max hit 16.5k to 17.1k, about 96 to 139 div.

**Review & improve** ([`BuildReview`](../src/Classes/BuildReview.lua); button beside Generate, and opened by itself after every generation or upgrade unless "Ask after every build" is off): the open build's key stats (damage, max hit, avoidance, clear, movement speed, attack / cast speed, life, energy shield, armour, evasion, block, recovery) each marked too low, fine or too high, with an optional note. "Improve this build" upgrades it with the focus moved by the review: weights of stats marked too low raised (x1.6 + 0.25; a goal the focus didn't have is added), too high lowered (x0.6), and a max hit marked too low raises the max hit floor to a quarter above it. Our Cyclone of Tumult, reviewed "max hit too low, movement speed too low": max hit 17k to 21.7k, EHP 44k to 78k, for 6.5M to 5.1M DPS.

Every review is kept (BuildIdeasReviews.json in the settings folder) and learnt from: each stat has a factor that moves 15% towards what the verdict asks (a "fine" pulls it back a little towards 1; between 1/3 and 3), and with "Use what reviews taught" on, every generation's focus is multiplied by those factors ("Learnt from your reviews: ehp x1.32, move x1.15" in the log).

**Variations** (button beside Review; `BuildGenerator:Variation`): four versions of the open build, each leaning one way: more damage (damage weight x2, tankiness x0.4), tankier (tankiness x4, avoidance x2, damage x0.5), faster (movement speed and attack / cast speed added as goals), and half the cost (fitted under half its cost). For each, the supports are chosen again, a third of the passives freed and spent again, uniques and rares tried again and a few repair rounds run. They are shown side by side with the build as it is (damage, max hit, EHP, movement speed, cost); the one picked opens, and the choice teaches the generator's weights like reviews: every stat where the pick is clearly higher than another option moves its factor up 5%, lower down 5% (`BuildReview.LearnChoice`; a `"type":"choice"` line in the decision log).

**A second skill for bosses**: a build made for clearing ("Clearing maps", "Clearing + bossing") tries the skills that share the main skill's damage types (attack or spell) in the 4-link with the fewest utility gems, on a pinnacle boss: alone first, then the best four with their own supports. When one beats the main skill on a boss by 30% it is linked there (or only suggested, when what it displaces is worth more).

Rares follow the budget: below Endgame investment, a rare has only the good natural mods a player can realistically craft or buy for it (3 at League start, 4 at Budget and Mid, 5 at High; plus the bench craft).

The configuration choice "Clearing + bossing" configures and scores the build on a pinnacle boss (damage, the boss fight, max hits: the build opens with that configuration), while what only matters while mapping still counts: clear speed weighs more, and Eldritch implicits are also measured while mapping (killed recently, normal enemies), that score added at a third.

Headhunter's stolen rare modifiers are counted as a typical set (40% increased damage, 20% attack and cast speed, 20% movement speed) behind a configuration switch ("Headhunter: rare modifiers stolen?"): on while mapping, off against a boss. For a build made to clear ("Mapping", "Clearing + bossing", or the clear speed focus) the belt still counts for clear speed while the boss numbers stay without it.

Enemy debuffs the build keeps up are switched on in the configuration like ailments: intimidate, unnerve, blind, maim, hinder, ash, frost, crush, scorch, brittle, sap (a line that inflicts it on hit, with its chance, at the main skill's hit rate), and exposure from Wave of Conviction or Frost Bomb.

Supports that bring a damage source of their own are kept to the main skills they fit: Decay only for chaos skills that aren't attacks, Summon Phantasm only for minion skills (with white gear at the start, Decay's fixed damage beat a weak wand attack and the whole build then scaled Decay), and Culling Strike never (top players get it from another skill that hits). Channelling skills move with Frostblink or Flame Dash, never with a travel skill that stops the channel. The first curse is one that adds damage; Enfeeble, Temporal Chains and Warlord's Mark only come with more curse slots.

Curses are tried on every way to apply them, each with what it costs in play (PoB counts a curse in full however it is applied): Arcanist Brand and casting by hand take time from the main skill, Hextouch on a movement skill only curses what it hits, Blasphemy reserves mana, a mark on Mark On Hit is free for attacks.

#### Knowledge from earlier generations
[`GeneratorKnowledge`](../src/Modules/GeneratorKnowledge.lua) keeps, in the user folder, what generations on this computer found out:

- **Steps** (GeneratorKnowledge.json): for each kind of build (investment level; attack, spell or minion), each optional late step's record: how often it improved the build, by how much in total, how long it took. A step that improved none of the last 8 builds of that kind is skipped (and tried again every fifth build, in case builds changed); the log says which ones. FinalRares, OptimizeTree and RepairSearch are measured but never skipped.
- **Builds** (GeneratorKnowledge.json): the best three builds made for each skill, ascendancy, budget, focus and configuration, whole (`SerializeState`; 60 builds at most). A new generation is crossed with the best two of them in a short population search, so it ends at least as well as the best earlier one even when it took a worse path (generations are path dependent: our Endgame Winter Orb of the previous version scores 17.06 under the current scorer, the current version's own 16.71). Older entries without the whole build give their parts (gear, main link, passives) like the top players' builds. Upgrades are remembered too.
- **Decisions** (GeneratorDecisions.jsonl): every choice a generation measured, for training models of the generator's choices. One JSON object per line; a `"type":"run"` line first with the build's context and result, then that run's decisions:
  - run: `id`, `v` (format version), `mode` (generate, quick, upgrade), `skill`, `tags` (the main skill gem's tags), `class`, `ascendancy`, `level`, `investment`, `budget`, `focus` (`id`, `dps`, `ehp`, `avoid`, `clear`, `hitFloor`, `goals`), `config`, `archetype`, `variant`, `seconds`, `final` (`score`, `dps`, `ehp`, `minHit`, `move`), `before` (upgrades), `decisions` (count).
  - decision: `run`, `k` (kind), `n` (what), `g` (score gain measured), `kept` (true when applied), `undone` (applied inside a trial that was rolled back), `step` (the generator step or stage it happened in), plus per kind: `support` (`pick`: which link slot was being chosen; every support tried at every pick, the chosen one with `kept`), `passive` (`id`, `type`, `points`, `asc`, `g` = gain per point, `alt`: the four best alternatives it beat, with their values), `unique_screen` (the 20 best uniques of the quick screen, with `slot`), `unique` (tried for real: `slot`, `price`, `kept`), `rare` (each rare made: base, `slot`, `mods`, `g` = its gain over the slot's previous item), `step` (`g` = score gained by the whole step, `sec`, `before`, or `skipped`), `note` (every log line, which names what each step changed and its gain).
  - review: a `"type":"review"` line for each review of a build (Review & improve): `skill`, `tags`, `ascendancy`, `level`, `focus`, `values` (each reviewed stat), `verdicts` (-1 too low, 0 fine, 1 too high), `note`.
  The file is renamed .old past 300 MB. Background threads send theirs back with their result; the main thread writes everything.

**Candidate model** ([`GeneratorModel`](../src/Modules/GeneratorModel.lua), trained in PoB by `GeneratorModelTrainer` (`bench/train_model.lua`, LOGS="a.jsonl;b.jsonl") or by `bench/train_model.py` with `--gems` from `bench/export_gem_types.lua`): for each support gem, a logistic regression on the main skill's gem tags and skill types ("t:"), the skill itself ("s:") and the build's situation at that choice ("c:": mana sustain, crit, damage types, ailments, links, investment) giving the chance it helps; and, per skill, the supports that never helped it in two builds or more. A support is skipped only when both agree, and only for skills seen in at least two builds. Neither is safe alone: the model alone missed Awakened Swift Affliction for an igniting Ice Nova at 0.5%, the history alone missed Lifetap for Tornado Shot (it helps when mana is short) 116 times; together, nothing. It is validated the way it is used: builds split in five groups (twice, differently), each left out in turn, every left-out build of a known skill judged by a model that never saw it, on the measurements that carry the build's situation (the oldest logs, from before it was recorded, are only trained on); the threshold kept is the highest where no skipped support would have gained more than 0.02. With 455,000 measurements of 104 skills (42 seen in two builds or more): 1% kept, about 4% of all support calculations skipped, and more as more skills become familiar (A/B on Ice Nova below). Training takes about 3.5 minutes.

**The build's situation as features** (`BuildGenerator:SupportContext`, a `support_ctx` decision before each support choice; support decisions carry its `cx`): mana sustain below 98% (`sus:low`), mana tight for the cost (`mana:tight`), a life cost, crit chance (high / mid / low), the damage types making a quarter of the hit (`dmg:Fire`...), damage over time over half the damage (`dot`), ignite / poison / bleed, fast skills, the link size and the investment level. The model sees them as "c:" features, so it can learn why a support helps (Lifetap when mana sustain is low) and not only that it did somewhere.

A remembered build saves a bad path: our second Arc generation (another variant) ended 0.85 below the first; crossed with it, it kept the first one's build.

**Training in PoB** ([`GeneratorModelTrainer`](../src/Modules/GeneratorModelTrainer.lua)): every 10 new generations in the decision log, the window trains the model again in the background (about 3.5 minutes for 455,000 measurements), with the same check (builds in five groups, two splits, model and history agreeing), and writes it to the user folder's GeneratorModel.json, which comes before the shipped one. The log says what it found ("Candidate model trained on ... no skip threshold was safe yet, so it only ranks").

**Unique pairs** (`unique_pair` decisions, `GeneratorModel.PairSynergy`): every pair of uniques the combo search measures is logged with both gains alone and together; the trainer keeps each pair's mean synergy, and the combo search ranks the pairs worth a calculation (400 at most) with it, so pairs earlier generations found working together are tried first.

**The build's own memory of supports** (`ChooseSupports(n, true)`): the full support choices of a generation (the supports stages, FinalSupports) try every support and remember which ones helped this main skill; the follow-up choices after small changes (repair rounds, Pearl of Tsoatha, the main link moved to a 4-link, sockets fitted) only try those, plus any never measured, once two full choices were made. `options.supportMemory = false` tries them all.

**Learnt uplift by kind** (`GeneratorLearning.Uplift(archetype, context)`): the full build's gain over the quick one is learnt per archetype and, within it, per kind of skill (attack, spell, minion) and investment level; the kind's mean is shrunk to the archetype's, which is shrunk to the global one, so few builds of a kind move it little.

#### Population search and combined tries
A generation follows one path: each step improves the build it has, so where it ends depends on where it started (tries of the same build come out up to a few points apart). [`PopulationSearch`](../src/Classes/BuildGenerator.lua) keeps several builds instead: each generation every member is partly rebuilt (`Perturb`: the tree's weakest branches pruned and re-spent, one or two rares made again, two supports chosen again), and the best member is crossed with each other one both ways (`Graft`: one's passives with the other's gear, its gear, or its main link; the tree then adjusted); the best ones that differ from each other (passives and gear, `stateDistance`) go on. Builds are carried as data (`CaptureState` / `ApplyState`: tree, every slot's item text, socket groups, configuration), and between threads as JSON (`SerializeState`).

With several tries, the tries are combined at the end (`Combine`, in the window and `bench/combine_test.lua`): the best try is opened and crossed with the others (with background threads, the three next best builds of any archetype within 1.5 points). Our Endgame Cyclone of Tumult crossed with the previous version's build: 19.33 to 19.40, 36M to 40M DPS (the gear of one with the tree of the other). `options.population` also runs it at the end of a single generation.

#### Several tries
"Tries" generates the same build several times with small differences: from the second try on, the early choices (supports, passives per point, ascendancy, weapon finalists) see each option's gain through a fixed ±15% factor of its own, which sends the search down other paths; the real scores still decide, and the best try is kept. With background threads the tries run at once.

The same inputs always give the same build: passive nodes are walked in id order and equal gains are compared to 1e-9 with fixed tie-breaks (LuaJIT changes the order of `pairs()` from run to run).

#### Several processor cores
[`ParallelGenerator`](../src/Classes/ParallelGenerator.lua) runs work in background threads, each with its own headless Path of Building ([`GeneratorWorker`](../src/Modules/GeneratorWorker.lua)):

- the archetype scan builds several quick builds at once;
- the automatic mode builds the best three archetypes at once;
- "Compare with top players" scores the strongest poe.ninja builds and takes them apart at once;
- with three or more workers (six cores), a generation in the app shares the gear slots out between threads for each pass of rare items.

Each worker holds its own copy of the game data (about 1 GB), so at most 3 run together by default (2 below six cores); `main.generatorWorkers` overrides it. Without background threads (headless runs, inside a worker) everything runs in one thread as before.

#### Tuning
`bench/tune.py` tries generator settings (`GEN_OPTS`: lensWeight, pastTarget, fightWeight, avoidanceWeight, ehpWeight...) on a set of cases and scores every build with the same reference scorer (`bench/rescore.lua`), so settings that change how the search scores can be compared.

#### Measuring a change
The [benchmark](../bench/README.md) generates fixed cases headless and compares them with an earlier run. Because generation is deterministic, two runs of the same code give the same builds, and a difference between two versions is the change's effect on that case (builds are path dependent, so judge a change on several cases). `GEN_OPTS="name=value,..."` switches generator options for A/B runs; `stage_test.lua` runs one step on a saved build; `profile_gen.lua` times every method; `eval_build.lua`, `ablate_build.lua` and `transplant.lua` compare generated builds with top players' builds; `penalty_census.py` counts which penalties fire on top players' builds (calibration).

Profiling: `luajit -jp=F40rm0,prof.txt ../bench/bench_case.lua ...` writes LuaJIT's sampling profile with whole call stacks; summing the samples by the outermost generator method shows where a generation spends its time. On a Mid Spark build (October 2026): rares 22% (made at the start and again for the finished build), the rebuild-and-repair rounds 16%, ideas of other archetypes 7%, ailment pairing 6%, tree allocation 6%, final supports 6%. Almost all of it is PoB's calculator (one full score is about 0.15 s, one quick score 0.01 to 0.05 s). Two fixes took 10% off: Pearl of Tsoatha candidates scored as a ring with only its two lines (parsing the full item, with hundreds of variants, for every support tried was 8% of a generation), and the repair rounds no longer reconfigure the build when the previous round already did.

The "Uniques (forced/excluded)" button beside the archetype opens a list where uniques can be excluded (never used: too rare, too expensive, not owned) or forced (equipped early in the slot where they score best, jewels in the nearest free socket, and the build made around them, whatever their price or rules). The choices are kept between sessions (BuildIdeasUniques.json in the settings folder) and also apply to the Build Ideas search. Stranglegasp is excluded unless forced: its value is its four anoints, which have to be found already on it.

"Aim for: Custom mix..." sets the build's own goals: weights for damage, tankiness, avoidance and clear speed, plus movement speed, attack / cast speed, life, energy shield, armour, evasion, block, recovery and area of effect (BuildScorer.goalList). Each goal counts as weight x log(stat): with weight 1, doubling the stat is worth as much as doubling the damage. Damage keeps at least 0.1. The weights are kept between sessions (BuildIdeasGoals.json). For example "fast and tanky" (damage 0.5, tankiness 1, avoidance 0.8, clear 0.3, movement speed 1.5) made a quick Cyclone Slayer with 1.55x movement speed and an 18.9k max hit instead of 0.80x and 13k, for a little less damage.

Ideas for more damage (October 2026):
- Damage behind conditions (Classes/ConditionAudit): every condition the build's modifiers use and that is off ("while on Full Life", "against Maimed enemies"...) is switched on for a moment and measured; for the big ones, the uniques and passives that grant the condition outright are tried and kept when the build is better. Unique pairs where one grants a condition outright and the other pays off while it holds are also tried (UniqueComboFinder.DataStates).
- Stacking ideas mined from the data (IdeaMiner.DataDrivers): every stat some uniques turn into damage ("per X" and "per Multiplier" modifiers) becomes an idea with those uniques and the uniques that turn another stat into it (accuracy, abyss jewels, elder items, intensity...). Conversion ideas (IdeaMiner.Conversions): uniques converting or adding damage as one element, with that element's aura and penetration.
- Hybrids: the automatic mode also builds the two best archetypes together ("Hybrids" box; BuildArchetypes.Hybrid, ids "A+B" in the benchmark).
- Rares: no Eldritch implicits on influenced items (older builds are fixed when their rares are made again; bench/rare_audit.lua checks saved builds), up to three bench crafts with "Can have up to 3 Crafted Modifiers" at Endgame and Mirror.
- Gems and corruption at Endgame and Mirror: the main skill at 23% quality, and each gear item gets the corrupted implicit that helps most ("+1 to Level of Socketed Gems", "+1 to Maximum Frenzy Charges"...), noted as a Vaal Orb on a spare copy.
